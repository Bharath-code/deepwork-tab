DeepWork Tab vs Freedom

Updated August 2026

These get compared because they sit in the same aisle, but they solve different problems. Freedom decides which sites you can reach. DeepWork Tab decides how many things you can have open. If you pick by category rather than by problem, you'll buy the wrong one.

The short version

If your problem is that you keep going to a specific site — Twitter at 10am, Reddit after lunch, the same three offenders every day — you want a blocker. Freedom does that across every device you own, and does it well.

If your problem is that you have 41 tabs open, none of them are the one you're working on, and you can't remember opening most of them — a blocklist won't touch that. Every one of those tabs is a site you'd have allowed. That's the case DeepWork Tab is for.

Why a blocklist misses the tab problem

Research at 3pm and doomscrolling at 3am look identical to a blocklist. Same domain, same URL, same everything. The difference is how many things you already had open when you went there — and a blocklist has no access to that number.

This is why people who use blockers still end up with forty tabs. The blocker is doing its job perfectly; the tabs just aren't on the list. Nobody's blocklist contains their own documentation, their own ticket tracker, or the Stack Overflow answer they opened nine minutes ago and never read.

A cap works on a different axis. It doesn't care what the site is. It cares that you already have seven things open and are about to make it eight, and it asks you — once, calmly, in your own words — whether that's what you meant to do.

The escape hatch is the real difference

Freedom's Locked Mode is stronger than our delay gate. If you need something that you genuinely cannot undo at 2pm on a bad day, Freedom wins that comparison outright and we're not going to pretend otherwise.

Our gate is 60 seconds. That's it. What it does during those 60 seconds is show you the sentence you typed when you installed it — the reason you wanted this in the first place, in your handwriting, not ours. Tightening the cap is instant; loosening it always waits. The friction is deliberately asymmetric.

That's a bet: that most tab-opening isn't a decision you'd defend, it's a reflex, and a reflex loses to sixty seconds and your own words. It's a weaker lock and a better mirror. Whether that's the trade you want depends on whether you're fighting a habit or fighting yourself.

Use Freedom instead if

  • You need your phone covered. This is a Chrome extension and will never be an app — it's on a written list of things this product refuses to become.
  • You want recurring schedules — block social media 9 to 5, every weekday.
  • You want a lock you can't talk your way out of mid-session.
  • Your problem really is a small number of named sites.

Use DeepWork Tab instead if

  • Your tab count is the number that embarrasses you, not your browser history.
  • You've tried blockers and ended up with forty allowed tabs.
  • You want to close things without the fear of losing them — the queue exists so nothing is lost.
  • You'd rather nothing left your machine. There's no account, no sync, and no analytics; the source is public.
  • You want the habit part free, permanently, rather than as a trial.

They also work together

They're not mutually exclusive, and they don't overlap much. Freedom for the sites you keep returning to across every device; a cap for the pile that accumulates inside a single work session. If you're already paying for Freedom, the free core here costs you nothing to try alongside it.
